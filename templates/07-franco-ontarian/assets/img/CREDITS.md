# Photography — Boulangerie Tremblay

Thirteen photographs, exclusive to this template. Nothing here is in the shared
pool in `shared/img/`, and nothing here is used by another template.

Five are Unsplash stock under the [Unsplash licence](https://unsplash.com/license).
Eight are from Wikimedia Commons; six of those carry Creative Commons licences
with an attribution condition, and that attribution is **on every page**, in
the footer, not only in this file — see `photoCredits` in
`data/merchant.boulangerie-tremblay.json`. Every file was cropped, resized and
re-encoded, which those licences require saying.

All of it stands in for the merchant's own photography.

Each image is `<name>-lg.jpg` (≈1600px), `-md.jpg` (900px) and `-sm.jpg`
(480px).

## Why half of this is amateur photography

The other six templates are stocked almost entirely from Unsplash, and they
look it: one lighting style, one depth of field, one hand. This one cannot be.

There is no stock photography of a tourtière. Searching the English name on a
stock library returns a generic meat pie; searching "sugar pie" returns
pumpkin pie; "butter tart" returns nothing at all. These are real dishes
photographed mostly by the people who ate them, and the honest places to find
them are Wikimedia Commons and nowhere else.

So the product photographs are lit by a kitchen window or a flash, at
whatever angle the cook happened to hold the camera. They are shown as they
are — no filter, no colour grade, nothing pretending they were shot in one
session. Captioning a stock meat pie "tourtière" is what D16 bans, and a
mismatched set of true pictures is the lesser fault by a long way.

A merchant who replaces this with one afternoon of their own photography will
improve the template more than any CSS in it.

**`grand-pere` is cropped hard, on purpose.** The original is a plate on a
red gingham tablecloth. The concept doc bans gingham by name — this is not a
heritage gift shop — so the frame comes down to the plate and the syrup.
Nothing has been added or altered; there is simply less tablecloth.

### Two things these pictures are not

**`tarte-au-sucre` is a maple syrup pie photographed in Falmouth, Maine.** The
uploader says so. It is the same dish under a different flag — syrup, cream,
a deep shell, the top gone dark — and it is the closest honest photograph
found. It is not a picture of a Hearst kitchen, and nothing on the page says
it is.

**The sugar pies that Commons files under *tarte au sucre* are European.** The
two best-lit candidates are from the Nord of France and from Pérouges: a flat
brioche under sugar, a different pastry with the same name. Using one would
have been precisely the lie this template is about — two names, two things —
told backwards.

## Rejected, and why

- **Tourtière plated at a table** — a stranger's whole place setting in shot,
  with people's hands. Not ours to publish.
- **Butter tarts in a supermarket tray** — honest, and it looks like a
  supermarket tray.
- **Lattice "berry" pies from Unsplash** — the filling photographs red. Calling
  a cherry pie *tarte aux bleuets* is D16's exact prohibition.
- **Pouding chômeur for `grand-pere`** — a different dessert. Close, Québécois,
  and still not the thing on the shelf.
- **A loaded pastry case** (`photo-1705972018470-a89eda1c6ce4`) — downloaded,
  placed on *Find us*, and pulled back off it: the price tags read in a
  currency that is not Canadian, on a page that names rue Principale, Hearst.
  The template would have been arguing with its own photograph.
- **A sugar shack in the bush** (`photo-1788888885856-d0d2c8b5aa05`) — the
  crowd outside it is identifiable, and no page needed it. Downloaded, looked
  at, deleted.

Every one of these was looked at on a contact sheet and then again in the
page, at size, in both languages.
