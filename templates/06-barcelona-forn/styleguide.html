1 · The separation method

Hydraulic floor tiles — rajoles hidràuliques — are in every old shop in Barcelona. They are square, they repeat, and they butt against one another: the join is the only division there is. No frame, no shadow, no card.

So content sits in cells that touch, and where one thing ends and the next begins is told by the change of surface plus the hairline of grout. Nothing in this template is drawn around anything. The two exceptions are both deliberate and both commented in the CSS: the drinks card and the totals list, where a price is read across a gap and the eye needs a line to cross it.

Each template in the set has its own: hairline rules and cards (01), drawn outlines and offset shadow (02), masked apertures (03), overlap and visible grid (04), ruled rows on pinned paper (05), and these.

3 · Type

A slab for everything that is painted on — headings, prices, labels, buttons — and a humanist sans for everything that is read. Barcelona shopfronts are lettered by hand, and painted letters carry their weight in the slab rather than in stroke contrast, which is also what keeps this clear of Template 01's high-contrast display serif.

5 · Photography

D16: the photography never lies about the product. Six of the baked things and three of the drinks carry a photograph. The rest do not, because no honest photograph of them exists — searching for an ensaïmada returns Mallorcan beaches, and a generic ring cake captioned Tortell de Reis would be the template lying about the one subject it is built on.

Those tiles take a plain second surface instead. Three earlier attempts filled the space with the tiled floor at various strengths and every one read as an image that had failed to load. A hole is only a hole if something is supposed to be in it. assets/img/CREDITS.md lists every file and its source.

Images are square-edged and flush, like every other surface: no radius, no shadow, no border. The hero is never lazy and its src is in the markup, not painted on by JavaScript.

6 · The year

The mechanic the template exists for. js/year.js is pure and DOM-free, and Easter is computed with the anonymous Gregorian computus because three of the shop's biggest days hang off it — a hardcoded date would be wrong every year but one, and silently.
