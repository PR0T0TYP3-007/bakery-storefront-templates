Template 05 — The Modern Neighbourhood Bakery

Style guide

Every component and every state, including the ones you would otherwise only catch on the right day — sold out, nearly gone, fully booked, a product with no photograph.

Colour

Status is never carried by colour alone — every state has a word beside it, and the sold-out marker is a square where the others are circles.

Type

Bricolage Grotesque (variable, wdth 92 / opsz 48) for display, Public Sans for interface. Both self-hosted. Body never below 16px. A grotesque rather than a serif, deliberately: it is what keeps this template from reading as the same identity as Template 01.

Buttons

Two radii in the whole template: 4px for rectangles, 999px for chips and the basket badge only. Every control is at least 44px tall, including the small ones.

Stock states

A live count only for today; batch size for any day still to come. Claiming "15 left next Tuesday" would be invented precision.

28 left 2 left Sold out 2 days notice

The board

The primary language. Rows hold their shape against a missing photograph, a 60-character name, a 3-item catalogue or a 300-item one.

Cake tiles

The only place photography leads, because a celebration cake is bought with the eyes and a baguette is not.

Loading

Skeleton rows reserve the exact final height, so nothing shifts when data lands (CLS).

Form fields

Visible labels always; placeholders are examples, never labels. Errors sit beside the field, carry an icon as well as colour, and say how to fix it.

As it should appear on the bag.

Options

Ruled rows, like the board. A combination we cannot make is disabled and says why — a dead control with no explanation is a dead end.

Elevation

Separation comes from 1px rules and ground-tone shifts. Shadow is reserved for three genuinely floating things: the mobile ticket sheet, the dialog, the toast. That constraint is what stops the page reading as a pile of tiles.
