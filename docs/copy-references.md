# Copy references

Where the writing in these five templates comes from, what was taken from each source, and
what was deliberately avoided.

**Nothing here is copied text.** No sentence, phrase or headline from any site below appears
in the templates. These are *voice* references: they were read to decide how plainly to
write, what to state outright, and what register to avoid. Quotes in this document are short
excerpts used to characterise each source, which is what makes the comparison checkable.

Lifting a real shop's copy would be plagiarism, would expose whoever ships these templates to
a copyright claim, and would not survive the first client who searched for a sentence of it.

**All sources below were read on 22 October 2026.** Where a page could not be reached that
day, it says so.

---

## 1. What was wrong with the copy before

The brief said not to write like an AI. The useful version of that note is not a style
preference — it is five specific habits, each of which appeared more than once across the set.

| Tell | Example as written | Why it reads as machine-made |
|---|---|---|
| **The site praising its own honesty** | "It is also why the diary is the honest part of this site" (T04); "It is also why the calendar on this site tells you the truth" (T01); "We would rather it did" (T02) | A shop has no reason to review its own website. This is the voice of a portfolio piece explaining why it is clever. It appeared in **three** templates independently, which is what made it diagnosable rather than a one-off. |
| **Antithesis as a default rhythm** | "before you pay, not after"; "That number is not a marketing position — it is…"; "That is why the diary is short and the notice is long" | "Not X, but Y" is a real device. Used as the house cadence it becomes a tic, and the reader starts hearing the pattern instead of the point. |
| **Tautological-poetic constructions** | "what is on it is what there is"; "what we have is what we baked this morning" | Sounds like folk wisdom, carries no information. Twice on one page. |
| **Rule-of-three ending on an abstraction** | "Piped, drizzled, unrestrained" | The first two terms describe the cake. The third describes a mood, and is there for the rhythm. |
| **Aphorism in place of a fact** | "This is the part that cannot be hurried" | The fact — it proves for two hours — is both shorter and more convincing. |

The replacement rule for all five: **state the thing, with its number, and stop.** The copy
was already strong on concrete detail in places ("cooked for four minutes and no longer",
"driven flat", "still fudgy"); the rewrite extends that and removes the commentary around it.

## 2. What the market actually sounds like

Reading ten bakery and cake sites produced one finding worth more than any individual phrase:

> **Almost every bakery site is marketing-led at the page level and plain only at the product
> level.**

Neal's Yard Dairy shows both registers on a single page — a product described as *"showing the
lard bound outer-casing and crumbly textured paste"*, under a banner reading *"Whatever you are
celebrating, we have the selection for you."* The specific writing is reserved for the thing in
the case; the page around it reverts to category language.

That gap is the opportunity, and it is what these templates now do: **plain at both levels.**
The logistics, the lead times and the shop's own limits are written with the same specificity
as the ingredients.

## 3. Positive references — what was taken

### ECBG Studio, Chicago — `ecbgstudio.com`
Required by the client as a reference for Template 03.

- Custom-inquiry page: *"Our custom cakes start at $300 and require a minimum of two weeks
  notice."* — minimum and notice period in one sentence, before any form.
- FAQ headings are phrased as a customer would say them, not as a business would file them:
  *"Can I eat the cake right away?"*, *"What is your minimum for wedding cakes?"*
- Terms carry deadlines: *"The nonrefundable 50% deposit is due within one week of
  confirmation."*
- A named human owns the process — a "Custom Cake Manager", by first name.
- It is not po-faced about it: the studio calls itself *"The It Girl of Chicago Cakes"*.

**Taken:** the habit of stating money and notice in the open, early, in one sentence.
**Used in:** Template 04's commission copy (`€280 minimum, 30% to book, holds for 14 days`,
repeated wherever a price is shown rather than hidden in terms); Template 03's cake pages;
Template 05's cutoff line. Also the FAQ phrasing across 03 and 04.
**Not taken:** the enquiry-and-wait model itself. Template 04 exists to do the opposite — see
`docs/04-creative-studio.md` §3.

### Neal's Yard Dairy — `nealsyarddairy.co.uk`
- Product copy names the material and its texture rather than the experience of eating it.

**Taken:** write the noun, not the adjective. Name the thing on the bench.
**Used in:** Template 01's product notes, Template 04's technique vocabulary ("Sugar heated and
pulled into shapes by hand", "A steel frame inside the cake so it can lean or overhang"),
Template 05's morning times (oven to 250, two hours proving).

### Toklas, London — `toklaslondon.com/bakery`
- Product copy is warm and adjective-rich; the opening hours and contact details beside it are
  flat and terse. The two registers sit on one page without blending.

**Taken:** the separation. Operational text does not get a voice.
**Used in:** every template's delivery, hours, allergen and cutoff lines, which are written
deliberately flatter than the surrounding copy.

## 4. Negative references — what was avoided

These are read as a *list of things not to write*. Each is a real, successful business; the
point is register, not quality.

### Pophams Bakery — `pophamsbakery.com`
- *"rooted in community and craftsmanship"*, *"enrich life's everyday rituals"*, *"a genuine
  sense of home"*, *"innovation and flavour to viennoiserie"*.

Aspirational abstraction: every phrase could belong to any food business in any country. This
is the exact register the brief's "no AI writing" note is pointing at — and notably, a human
wrote it. **Avoided throughout.** Template 05's "Before the doors" section is the direct
counter-example: instead of claiming craft, it gives three times and what happens at each.

### Tartine Bakery — `tartinebakery.com/about`
- *"Rise each day. Warm every table."*, *"It felt like fate."*

Slogan fragments and origin-story sentiment. **Avoided:** no template has a slogan, and the
founder stories that exist are occupational — who trained where, who came from bread.

### Bread Ahead — `breadahead.com`
- *"Learn to bake with the experts, loved by thousands."*, *"the home of quality British
  baking"*, *"Book your Christmas baking experience now!"*

Unverifiable social proof and exclamation-led calls to action. **Avoided:** no template claims
to be loved, famous or best. Template 02's "Nobody else lets you see it first" was removed in
this pass for exactly this reason — it was an absolute claim about every competitor.

### The Dusty Knuckle — `thedustyknuckle.com`
- *"Fresh Dusty drip in slick new colourways"*, *"Need gifts for your hardworking crew?"*

Slang and emoji as the brand voice. Works for them and is tied to a real youth training
programme; it is a strong voice and the wrong one to borrow, since a template has to survive
being used by a merchant who is not that shop.

### e5 Bakehouse — `e5bakehouse.com`
- *"Making food that is healthy, for ourselves and for the planet"*, *"e5's wonderful
  products"*.

Values-led origin storytelling. **Partly avoided:** the templates state facts that imply values
(one oven, three people, fourteen cakes a day) without naming the value.

## 5. Changes made in this pass

| Template | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | "That number is not a marketing position — it is the number that fits in the oven…" | "It is what fits in the oven, and what three people can finish by hand before the shop opens." |
| 01 | "It is also why the calendar on this site tells you the truth…" | "When a Saturday is full the calendar stops offering it, rather than taking the order and ringing you on the Thursday." |
| 02 | "Nobody else lets you see it first" / "Every other cake shop gives you a form and a surprise." | "See it before you order it" / "Most cake forms end in a surprise." |
| 02 | "Which is why the calendar says no sometimes. We would rather it did." | "When they are gone the calendar stops offering that day." |
| 03 | "…see it set in the hand it will be written in — before you pay, not after." | "…watch it set in the hand it will be written in while you are still deciding." |
| 03 | Two paragraphs on what other shops get wrong, including a line of competitor research | One paragraph on what this card physically does: three lines in this hand, and which word is the problem |
| 04 | "It is also why the diary is the honest part of this site…" | "Most weeks are taken before they start, and the calendar shows which ones." |
| 04 | "We looked at how this is normally done." | "Usually you fill in a form, pick a budget band, and wait for someone to email you." |
| 04 | "gold only where the light already lands" | "gold on the top edge only" |
| 05 | "what we have is what we baked this morning" | "We bake once, in the morning, and when a thing is gone it is gone until tomorrow." |

## 6. Two things found while rewriting, which are not style

**Fabricated press quotes attributed to real publications (Template 01).** The press section
carried invented praise credited to *The New Yorker*, *Cherry Bombe*, *Eater NY* and *Kinfolk*
— four real outlets, none of which has written about a bakery that does not exist. That is
survivable in a mock-up and not in a template, because demo content ships: the most plausible
line is the one a merchant is most likely to leave in place, and the resulting false
endorsement would be the merchant's legal problem. The sources now read "Press quote —
replace", with a comment in `catalogue.js` explaining why they must stay invented.

**A possessive apostrophe bug (Template 02).** `cards.js` appended `'` to the lead-time word
unconditionally, so every product needing one day printed "1 day' notice". Fixed by building
the whole phrase in one place, since "Today" takes no apostrophe and "1 day's" takes a
different one from "4 days'".
